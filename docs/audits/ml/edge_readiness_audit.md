## Part 3: Bias Audit

An analysis of signal directions across the entire history of the platform indicates direction distribution and potential bias:

### 1. All Historical Signals (N = 22,750)
*   **Long Signals:** 11,041 (48.53% of total) | Average Confidence: 82.57%
*   **Short Signals:** 8,765 (38.53% of total) | Average Confidence: 68.97%
*   **Neutral Signals:** 2,944 (12.94% of total) | Average Confidence: 58.62%

*Evidence: There is a notable long bias in the historical signal generation (approx. 1.26x more long signals than short signals), accompanied by significantly higher average confidence scores for long signals.*

## Part 4: Calibration Audit

To determine if the confidence values generated by models are meaningful, we analyze how they correlate with outcome win rates. Due to the small sample size of the ground truth schema, we utilize the reconstructed signal population (N = 19,785) to analyze bucket calibration:

| Confidence Bucket | Total Signals | Reconstructed Wins | Reconstructed Losses | Reconstructed Timeouts | Win Rate (Excl. Timeouts) | Win Rate (Incl. Timeouts) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **80%+** | 8,558 | 1,980 | 3,374 | 3,204 | 36.98% | 23.14% |
| **70-79%** | 5,306 | 725 | 1,782 | 2,799 | 28.92% | 13.66% |
| **60-69%** | 2,135 | 674 | 1,169 | 292 | 36.57% | 31.57% |
| **50-59%** | 2,155 | 468 | 298 | 1,389 | **61.10%** | 21.72% |
| **40-49%** | 1,137 | 17 | 768 | 352 | **2.17%** | 1.50% |
| **Under 40%** | 494 | 170 | 324 | 0 | 34.41% | 34.41% |

### Findings on Confidence Meaningfulness:
1.  **Non-Monotonicity:** There is no monotonic correlation between confidence score and actual win rate. For example, the 50-59% confidence bucket exhibits the highest win rate (61.10% excluding timeouts), while the highest-confidence 80%+ bucket yields a win rate of only 36.98%.
2.  **Calibration Failure:** The 40-49% bucket exhibits a near-zero win rate (2.17%), but confidence levels under 40% jump back to 34.41%.
3.  **Conclusion:** Confidence values in their current state are poorly calibrated and do not reliably represent probability of success.
